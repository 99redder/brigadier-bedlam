Build the first playable prototype of a browser game: a satirical, cartoonish
real-time squad tactics game inspired by the 1993 Sega Genesis game General
Chaos. This is a personal project. It is an original game in the same genre,
so use no names, sprites, music or other assets from the original. Working
title: "Brigadier Bedlam".

The game is single player only, against computer-controlled enemies. Do not
build or plan for multiplayer of any kind, local or online.

WHAT THE GAME IS
Two squads of 5 soldiers fight in real time on a single-screen battlefield
viewed from a 3/4 isometric-style angle. The battlefield has cover objects
that block direct fire, and patches of water. A battle ends when every
soldier on one side is down. The tone is slapstick: exaggerated cartoon
soldiers, comic deaths, nothing gritty.

Soldier classes (my names; keep all names and stats in one data file so I
can rename and rebalance easily):
- Rifleman: machine gun. Medium range, high rate of fire, all-rounder.
  The gun occasionally jams and he can't fire for a few seconds.
- Bazooka: rocket launcher. Longest range, slow to fire, blocked by cover.
- Lobber: grenades. Good range, low rate of fire, arcs over cover, damages
  everyone in a small radius.
- Torch: flamethrower. Shortest range, fastest firing, hits several
  soldiers at once, does not work while standing in water.
- Sapper: dynamite. Short range, very slow, arcs over cover, heavy damage
  in a small radius.

Other rules:
- A soldier whose health reaches zero falls down and stays on the field.
  The player can revive him by calling a medic, who runs on, revives him
  and runs off. Medic calls are limited per battle (start with 3).
- Bazooka, Torch and Sapper hits have a small random chance of an instant
  kill that leaves a skeleton or a pile of ash. Those soldiers cannot be
  revived.
- When two opposing soldiers get very close they enter close combat: both
  stop shooting and brawl with punch, kick and block until one is knocked
  down. Nearby soldiers are knocked back when a brawl starts.
- Before a battle the player picks one of three preset squads with
  different class mixes. The enemy gets a random one.

CONTROLS
Every action goes through a rebindable input layer. Game code asks "was
the Punch action triggered", never "was the left mouse button pressed".
Both keyboard keys and mouse buttons can be bound to any action.

Default layout, designed for mouse in the right hand and the left hand
resting on the WASD cluster:

Mouse
- Left click a friendly soldier: select him.
- Left click ground: selected soldier moves there.
- Left click an enemy: selected soldier attacks him, moving into range
  first if needed.
- Left click a downed friendly: call a medic.
- Right click: deselect. Suppress the browser context menu over the game.

Keyboard (left hand)
- 1-5: select soldier by squad slot.
- Q / E: select previous / next living soldier.
- R: call a medic for the downed friendly nearest the cursor.
- F: selected soldier holds position and stops chasing his target.
- Space: pause.
- Esc: open the menu (also pauses).
- W A S D: leave unbound in this build. They are reserved for direct
  movement in the future two-man commando mode, so don't assign them to
  anything else by default.

Close combat, when the player's selected soldier is in the brawl
- Left click: punch.
- Right click: kick.
- Left Shift: block (held).
Brawls between soldiers the player is not controlling resolve
automatically.

Unselected soldiers look after themselves: they fire at enemies in range
and hold position otherwise.

The core loop of select, move, attack and revive must stay playable with
the mouse alone under the default layout. The keyboard defaults are
shortcuts, except block, which needs a key or a rebind to a mouse button.

Controls settings screen
- Reachable from the title screen and the in-game menu.
- Lists every action with its current binding. Click a binding, then
  press a key or mouse button to change it.
- If the new binding is already used by another action in the same
  context, warn and offer to swap. Battlefield actions and close combat
  actions are separate contexts, so left click can be both "select/move"
  and "punch".
- Allow a second, alternate binding per action.
- A "Reset to defaults" button, and a "Left-handed mouse" preset that
  mirrors the keyboard defaults to the right side of the keyboard
  (6-0, U / O, P, J, Right Shift).
- Bind by physical key position (KeyboardEvent.code), so the layout
  stays under the same fingers on AZERTY, Dvorak and other layouts, but
  display the label the user's own keyboard prints for that key.
- Don't allow binding keys the browser needs, such as F5, F11, Tab, or
  anything combined with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd.
- Save bindings in localStorage and load them on start. If saved data is
  missing or invalid, fall back to the defaults silently.
- On-screen hints and the win/lose and pause screens must show the
  player's current bindings, not hard-coded key names.

SCOPE OF THIS FIRST BUILD
Build: a title screen, squad selection, one battlefield, all five classes,
cover and water, medics, instant kills, close combat, enemy AI, the
controls settings screen, a pause menu, and a win/lose screen that returns
to the title.

Do not build yet, but don't make them hard to add: a campaign played across
a strategic map of territories, more battlefields, a tutorial, a two-man
"commando" squad with direct control, pickups and scoring, and final art
and audio.

TECHNICAL DIRECTION
- TypeScript, Vite, and Phaser 3. It must build to a static site with no
  backend.
- Keep the battle simulation (movement, targeting, damage, AI, brawls,
  medics) in plain TypeScript modules that do not import Phaser, driven by
  a fixed timestep and a seedable random number generator. Phaser only
  draws the simulation state and forwards input. This is so the rules can
  be unit tested without a browser, and so a battle can be replayed from
  a seed when I report a bug.
- Write unit tests with Vitest:
  - Simulation: weapon ranges and cooldowns, cover blocking direct fire
    but not arcing weapons, the flamethrower failing in water, revive and
    no-revive deaths, medic limits, and battle end conditions.
  - Input bindings: defaults load, a rebind persists and reloads,
    conflicts within a context are detected, and invalid saved data falls
    back to defaults.
- Art: generate simple placeholder sprites in code or as small SVGs.
  Each class must be recognisable at a glance by colour and silhouette,
  and the two sides clearly distinguishable. Soldiers need visibly
  different states for idle, moving, firing, brawling, down, and dead
  (skeleton or ash). Put all art behind one asset-loading module so I
  can swap in real sprite sheets later without touching game code.
- Audio: a few short synthesised sound effects are enough. No music yet.
- Target a 16:9 play area that scales to fit the browser window on a
  desktop. Mobile and touch are not needed.

ENEMY AI
The AI is what makes this fun or not, so give it real attention. Enemy
soldiers should pick targets sensibly for their weapon, keep to their
preferred range, use cover, avoid standing in water as a Torch, back off
when badly hurt, and call their own medics. It should be beatable by a
new player but punish leaving soldiers idle in the open. Put the AI's
tuning numbers in the same data file as the class stats.

HOW TO WORK
- Start by getting one soldier moving by mouse click on an empty
  battlefield, then add things in an order that keeps the game runnable
  at every step.
- Run the game in a browser yourself and play it as you go. Passing unit
  tests are not enough; check that selection, movement, shooting, brawls,
  medics and rebinding actually work, and that a full battle can be won
  and lost.
- Weapon stats and AI numbers will need tuning. Make a reasonable first
  pass, play several battles, and adjust so battles last roughly 2 to 4
  minutes and no class is useless or dominant.
- If a rule here is ambiguous, pick the option that is more fun and
  simpler, and note the choice.
